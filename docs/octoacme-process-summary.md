# OctoAcme Project Management Process Summary

## Overview
OctoAcme operates on a five-phase project lifecycle designed to deliver customer value through iterative, data-informed execution. Projects begin with **Initiation**, where business needs are validated and stakeholders aligned around a lightweight one-pager that defines the problem, objectives, and success metrics. This flows into **Planning**, where the team breaks work into shippable increments, estimates scope, identifies dependencies, and creates a detailed release plan. The core delivery happens during **Execution**, supported by daily standups, a structured pull request workflow with code reviews, and comprehensive testing practices. Once ready, projects move through **Release**, with staged deployments, smoke tests, and post-deploy verification. Finally, teams conduct **Retrospectives** to capture learnings and convert them into actionable improvements that feed back into the process. This lifecycle ensures clear visibility, manageable risk, and continuous refinement of team practices.

## Key Workflows and Roles
The OctoAcme framework defines three core roles that collaborate throughout the project lifecycle. The **Project Manager (PM)** coordinates delivery activities, manages schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication, ensuring projects stay on track and aligned. The **Product Manager (PdM)** owns the product vision, prioritizes the backlog, and measures outcomes against success metrics to maximize customer value. **Developers** implement features, write and maintain tests, collaborate on design decisions, and help identify technical risks. Supporting these roles are QA/Testing teams who validate acceptance criteria, and Stakeholders who provide approvals and strategic input. Work flows through a structured backlog using GitHub Projects with columns for Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. Pull requests follow lightweight conventions—kept to ≤400 lines when possible, linked to issues with clear acceptance criteria, and requiring at least one approval before merge. This clear ownership model and defined workflow eliminate ambiguity and accelerate decision-making across distributed teams.

## Communication and Risk Management
OctoAcme establishes a predictable communication cadence to maintain alignment and surface issues early. **Daily standups** (15 minutes) focus on progress, blockers, and dependencies at the team level, while **weekly delivery syncs** showcase progress and highlight flagged risks to the broader delivery team. **Weekly PM + PdM synchronization** ensures strategic alignment on priorities and outcomes, and **monthly stakeholder updates** keep sponsors and external partners informed. For escalations, OctoAcme uses a three-level path: team-level triage in standups, PM escalation to Product Lead and dependent teams, and sponsor-level escalation for business-impacting issues. Risk management is formalized through a **Risk Register** (maintained throughout execution) that tracks ID, description, impact, likelihood, owner, mitigation plan, and status. Risks are reviewed weekly during syncs and monitored continuously, with clear escalation paths for high-impact or high-likelihood risks. This transparent, cadence-based approach builds trust and enables teams to respond quickly to emerging challenges.

## Quality Assurance and Continuous Improvement
Quality is embedded throughout OctoAcme's execution model, not treated as an afterthought. Every pull request triggers automated **CI/CD pipelines** that run unit tests, integration tests, linting, and security scanning before human review. Developers write and maintain tests as part of their implementation; teams run end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows before release, and manual QA validates feature acceptance when needed. The **Definition of Done** is documented upfront during planning, ensuring consistent quality standards across all work. Beyond individual release cycles, OctoAcme institutionalizes learning through structured **Retrospectives** held after each sprint, release, or important milestone (timebox: 45–75 minutes). Retrospectives capture what went well, what could improve, and generate prioritized action items with owners and due dates. These improvements are tracked in the project backlog and reviewed weekly, creating a virtuous cycle of continuous refinement. This dual focus on real-time quality gates and systematic process improvement means OctoAcme teams deliver reliable software while steadily reducing friction and accelerating delivery speed.
